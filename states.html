What you hand over.

Two exports and one connection. Your office already produces all three.

Your referees

  • One export from the US Soccer Learning Center: names, licenses, clearances, contact details, date of birth.
  • Repeated whenever you like; a weekly load during registration season keeps the renewal count current.
  • Minors are flagged from the first load. Guardian contact is used in place of theirs wherever staff reach out.

Your games

  • Read access to your assigning platform. Assignr today; others on request.
  • Games sync every two hours, every assigning group, four weeks ahead. Nobody re-enters a schedule.
  • Referee names on games are matched to people once and remembered.

Your history, if you want the numbers

  • Past Learning Center exports, as far back as you have them. Colorado loaded 2012 to today in an afternoon.
  • This is what makes the State of the program screen: registration by year, who stays, who leaves, who comes back.

Your brand

  • Your badge, your program name, your colors. Referees see your program, not ours.
  • Your rules pages per competition, your bulletins, your contact line in the footer.

Questions a board will ask.

Where does our data live?

In a database under your program's own account, not ours. Access is by role, enforced in the database itself, not only in the app. If you ever stop, you keep everything.

Do we have to leave Assignr?

No. The Hub reads from it. Assignors keep working the way they do. Referees stop opening three apps.

What about minors?

Minors are flagged from the Learning Center data. Staff who reach out see the guardian's contact. Protection alerts reach the board. Nobody's phone location is tracked, for anyone.

What does the AI do, and what doesn't it do?

It tidies coaching notes for tone and clarity. A person reads every note before it goes to a referee, and a flagged note cannot go out without a rewrite. It does not rate referees, assign games or decide anything.

Who supports it on a Saturday?

The person who built it, by phone. Updates ship as files; your office never installs anything. The app is a web page that lives on the home screen of any phone.

What does it cost?

It's a conversation, not a rate card. States pick the tools they want and pay per program or per person. Tournaments pay by the event, referee coaches by the seat. Ask for a demo and bring a weekend's schedule.
