The referee taps Running late and a time. The coordinator acknowledges it from the board, the referee sees the acknowledgement, and the open slot is listed under Needs attention until someone covers it.

What's inside.

One app, one sign-in with an emailed code, one database you own. Each person sees the screens their role unlocks.

Every referee

Your day

Games, countdown, check-in, running late, the rules for each competition, the crew, coaching notes, the fitness test sign-up and your season's numbers. Works on iOS and Android as a home-screen app, in English or Spanish, and is built to go easy on the battery for people who are outside all day.

Site coordinators, operations

Command Center

Every venue and every crew, live. Needs attention first, then the full board. Bulletins to referees. Help calls. Weather. Incident reports. Protection alerts for minors.

Referee coaches

Coaching

Who's at my venue, who hasn't had feedback this season, one note per referee written on the phone. Coaches see each other's released notes, so the fourth note builds on the third.

Director of Referee Development

Review

Every note cleaned by AI and read by a person before release. Flagged notes are rewritten or held. Coverage: who has worked, who has been seen, who has read their notes.

Referees and evaluators

Evaluations

A referee requests a formal evaluation from their game screen, for maintenance or an upgrade. The evaluator writes it in the same app; it counts toward the referee's record.

Head of refereeing

State of the program

Fifteen years of registration from the Learning Center exports: referees by year, first-year versus returning, who comes back after a break, retention, age, gender, experience, compliance, and where referees live on a map with the fields pinned. Season numbers update live.

Operations

Setup

Events, their venues, rules pages and alert emails. Fitness tests, classes and clinics with sign-up, capacity and a roster. A bulletin goes out the moment a session is created.

Instructors

Fitness test

The FIFA test on one sheet: six sprints, the interval run by run with the pacer playing from the instructor's phone, CODA for assistant referees. Pass only when every part passes. Print or save the PDF. The referee gets a badge.

Operations, payroll

End of day

Coordinators and coaches file hours from the phone. Payroll by venue, date range or person; edit a report after filing; print.

Everyone

Reference Library

Laws of the Game, signals, procedures, career paths, the fitness standards with audio, and each competition's rules on its own page. Offline once opened.

Operations

People

Every referee from the Learning Center, matched to the assigning platform's names. Titles and roles. The list of who hasn't registered for next year, with emails ready to copy and a CSV for the assignors.

The program

Retention, measured

Registration tracked week by week through the renewal season. The Hub asks each referee to renew from their own screen, and stops asking the day their new license shows up.

The numbers a director never had.

This is Colorado's real chart, from the State of the program screen. Every year's roster is made of three groups. The program holds at 2,900 only because 1,200 new referees arrive each year to replace the ones who leave.

For your state. Or your tournament.

The Hub runs on your data, in your name, with your badge in the corner. Colorado's is the first; the next one takes weeks, not a season.

Week 1

Your people

Export your referees from the US Soccer Learning Center. Names, licenses, clearances and contact details load into your own database. Minors are handled under your protection rules from the first day.

Week 2

Your games

Connect your assigning platform. Games sync every two hours, every group, four weeks out. Referee names are matched to people once and remembered.

Week 3

Your weekend

Your coordinators, coaches and reviewers get their unlocks. Pick one venue for the first Saturday. The board is live, the notes flow, and by Sunday night you have your first coverage numbers.

The page for state directors covers what you hand over, what each role gets, and the first season. What you keep: your data, in a database under your account. What you get: the app, the updates, and the person who built it on the phone. What it costs is a conversation, not a rate card: states pick the tools they want and pay per program or per person; tournaments pay by the event; referee coaches by the seat.
